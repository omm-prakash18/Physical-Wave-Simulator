# 🌊 Latent Wave Prediction Studio: A Conceptual & Technical Guide

A portfolio-grade scientific ML forecasting engine demonstrating end-to-end 2D wave equations modeling, CNN compression, causal sequence attention, and numerical solvers alignment.

---

## 🎨 Part 1: The Non-Technical Overview (50%)

### 🔮 1. What is this Studio used for?

At its core, this project is an **AI-powered weather forecast for physical waves**. Just as a meteorologist predicts where a storm will travel by looking at recent satellite images, this studio takes a short video of wave ripples (like drops in a pond) and predicts how those waves will expand, interfere, and bounce off walls in the future.

> [!NOTE]
> Instead of running slow, traditional mathematical wave-equation solver calculations, this tool uses a trained **neural network (AI)** to predict the future in milliseconds.

---

### 💧 2. The Water Ripple Metaphor

Imagine dropping a stone into a still swimming pool:

* **The Past (Context)**: You watch the first 10 frames of the stone hitting the water and the initial circular ripple starting to spread out.
  
* **The Future (Forecast)**: The AI takes that short clip and instantly predicts the next 10 frames—showing how the ripples spread, hit the edges of the pool, bounce back, and cross path with each other.

While this studio simulates simplified 2D waves, the underlying technology is used in major industrial and scientific fields:

* 🌊 **Tsunami & Coastline Warning Systems**: Predicting how ocean waves propagate and intensify as they approach shorelines or harbors.

## 🛠️ Part 2: The Technical Deep Dive (50%)

### 📐 1. Neural Network Architecture

The system employs an end-to-end **Encoder-Decoder Causal Latent Transformer** pipeline:

1. **Residual CNN Encoder**: Compresses 10 context frames of shape `(B, 10, 1, 64, 64)` into 10 low-dimensional latent tokens $z \in \mathbb{R}^{256}$.
   
2. **Causal Transformer Decoder**: Processes $T_{\text{out}}$ learned query tokens in parallel, cross-attending back to context tokens to project the latent states of future frames.
   
3. **Residual CNN Decoder**: Decompresses the predicted future latent tokens back into raw grayscale frames `(B, 10, 1, 64, 64)`.

```
[Context Frames] ──> (CNN Encoder) ──> [Latent Tokens] ──┐
                                                         ▼
[Forecast Frames] <── (CNN Decoder) <── [Future Latents] <── (Transformer Decoder)
                                                                 ▲
                                                          [Query Tokens]
```

---

### 🧮 2. Physics-Based Data Generator

The ground truth waves are generated by solving the 2D finite-difference wave equation:

$$\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} = c^2 \left( \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2} \right)$$

Using a second-order central difference leapfrog scheme on a $64 \times 64$ grid. Boundaries can be set to reflecting (Neumann $\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=0$) or absorbing.

---

### ⚡ 3. Training & Systems Optimizations

We implement three core components to stabilize and optimize the training of this pipeline:

* **Learned Uncertainty Weighting (Kendall et al.)**: Loss terms (L1 Reconstruction, Latent MSE, Perceptual L1, and Temporal Consistency) are balanced dynamically during backpropagation via learned log-variance parameters:
  $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \sum_j \left( e^{-s_j} \mathcal{L}_j + \frac{1}{2} s_j \right)$$
  
* **Rollout Horizon Curriculum**: Training dynamically restricts the loss calculation steps in early epochs (starting at 3 frames, scaling up to 10), accelerating convergence.
  
* **Latent Space Probing**: Linear OLS regressions are trained on latents to predict physical variables, validating that the CNN Encoder linearly disentangles:

| Disentangled Variable | Validation $R^2$ Score | Physical Interpretation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pulse Center X** | **`0.9701`** | Coordinate grid mapping representation |
| **Pulse Center Y** | **`0.9651`** | Coordinate grid mapping representation |
| **Pulse Width ($\sigma$)** | **`0.9924`** | Gaussian scale representation |
| **Wave Amplitude** | **`0.0000`*** | Normalized out by per-sequence scaling |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> *Amplitude R² is exactly 0.0000 because sequences are normalized to `[-1, 1]` per-sequence during dataset simulation. This scales out the absolute amplitude scale, meaning the input pixel frames contain zero scale information, which is correctly reflected in the zero probe score.
